Models and agents

01AI · research

OpenAI published hundreds of math results from a model it has not shipped.

On 6 Oct, OpenAI posted a GitHub catalogue of results from an internal frontier model, after consulting the independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI at the Institute for Advanced Study. A close reading counted 722 manuscripts in 372 families, from a sweep of about 4,000 problems, roughly 3 hours of Pro-class compute each, with an explicit warning that unformalized results may still be wrong. The New York Times framed it as 377 findings across algebra, number theory, logic, and topology, following last month’s Navier-Stokes claim. Scientific American said 372 results, many Lean-checked, including a claimed 4D Kakeya solution and progress toward Riemann, produced mostly by one agent on one prompt — unlike the earlier 10,000-agent Navier-Stokes swarm.

Why it took off: a lab dropped a semester of open problems at 6 p.m. and dared the field to grade it overnight.

Sharpest forFrenchie: modern math may have started in 2024–26; the job moves from discovery to verification. The Lean slice is checkable in days.
Sharpest againstSciAm’s real question is whether these are new ideas or mash-ups. Fields-medal skepticism is that labs are racing open problems for the press cycle. Unformalized claims are not theorems.

For you: this is the verification layer the brief and studio pipelines still underbuild. Generation is cheap. A cited, retractable claim is the product.
